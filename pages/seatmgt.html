01 The problem

Spreadsheets don't know who moved. The floor does.

Seats are wired to specific network ports. Devices change hands. People take a second desk, lend a monitor, or quietly move across the room, and your tracking sheet is out of date the moment they do.

The cost isn't just a stale file. You don't know which port a machine is on, who owns what, or where the free seats actually are. Every reshuffle turns into a manual hunt, and nobody trusts the numbers.

02 The solution

A living floor plan, backed by rules.

SeatMGT keeps every room, seat, device and person as a real record. Each seat maps to exactly one network port, people get seats by request and approval, and devices are tracked to whoever owns them. What you see on the map is what's actually true.

One source of truth

One place to see who sits where and what's plugged into which port, always current.

Mistakes can't be saved

The app won't accept a double-booked seat or a device on the wrong desk. It doesn't just warn you; it stops the change.

Self-service with oversight

People request seats, managers approve, admins own the floor. Moves happen quickly and stay under control.

Rules SeatMGT keeps for you
01

One occupant per seat. A person can hold several seats, but a seat is never double-booked.

02

The primary device belongs to the occupant. A seat's primary device is always owned by whoever sits there.

03

Status comes from the occupant. Empty or occupied is worked out from who is assigned, so it can't fall out of sync.

04

Clean release when someone leaves. Leaving a seat releases that person's primary device from it. No leftover links.

03 How it works

From an empty desk to a tracked device, in four moves.

This is the everyday path. The rules do their work quietly in the background.

01

Request

Pick an open seat on the map and send a request. It waits in a queue for a manager.

02

Approve

A manager approves the request, or assigns someone directly. The seat now has its occupant.

03

Place device

Your machine becomes the seat's primary device, tied to that seat's network port.

04

Release

When you leave, the seat opens up and your primary device is released from it. Nothing is left dangling.
