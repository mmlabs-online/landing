The problem

A certificate spreadsheet is out of date the day after it's updated.

Certificates are earned, renewed and quietly expire. People finish a course and forget to update the sheet, statuses get typed five different ways, and the expiry date is something you work out yourself. By the time anyone notices a certificate has lapsed, it's too late to renew it on time.

The expiry nobody saw coming

A certificate a project relies on lapses, and the team finds out when someone asks for proof.

“Can you update the sheet?”

The data depends on one person chasing everyone else. It's never quite current, so nobody fully trusts it.

The monthly report by hand

“How many valid AWS certificates do we have?” should take a minute. Without one shared view, it means asking around and counting rows.

The solution

One shared record, with the rules built in.

Every person, course and certificate is a real record. Expiry is worked out for you, everyone updates their own progress with proof, and reminders arrive before it's too late.

One source of truth

Who holds which certificate, who is studying for what, and when each one expires. One place, always current.

Everyone updates their own

Members record their own progress and upload proof. Managers see the change straight away, without chasing anyone.

Warned in time

A Monday e-mail lists certificates expiring within 60 days, so renewals start while there's still time.

Rules CertTracker keeps for you

The checks a spreadsheet leaves to people's memory, done every time a record is saved.

  • Expiry is calculated, never typed. Issue date plus the course's validity gives the expiry date and status, so it can't drift.
  • One record per person per course. A renewal updates the same record; there are no duplicate rows.
  • Status and progress always agree. “Done” means 100% with an issue date; “Not started” means 0%. Anything else can't be saved.
  • No issue dates in the future. A certificate can't be marked as issued on a day that hasn't happened.
  • Proof is checked. PDF, JPG, PNG or WebP up to 4 MB, recognised by its content, not by its file name.

How it works

From the old spreadsheet to reminders that arrive on time.

The everyday path, in four steps. The rules do their work in the background.

  1. Import

    An admin uploads the existing Excel file. CertTracker cleans it, shows what will be created, updated or skipped, and imports it in one step.

  2. Update

    Members add new certificates, track their progress and attach proof. Managers can do the same for their team.

  3. Get reminded

    Every Monday, members and managers get an e-mail about certificates that expire in the next 60 days or have just expired.

  4. Report

    The dashboard and a monthly e-mail give managers and admins the numbers, ready to share or export.

Who it's for

Three roles, one clear line of control.

Everyone works from the same record. Your role decides what you can see and do.

Admin

Owns the record

Training or operations leads

  • Set up centres, programs, teams and the course catalogue
  • Add members and assign team managers
  • Import the old spreadsheet and export data
  • See everything, including data-quality checks and e-mail history
Manager

Keeps the team certified

Team and project leads

  • See every certificate in the teams they manage
  • Get the Monday reminder for their teams and the monthly report
  • Update records for their team members
  • See the team breakdown and personal ranking on the dashboard
Member

Keeps their own certificates current

Everyone on the team

  • Add certificates and track study progress
  • Upload proof for each certificate
  • Get a reminder before their own certificates expire
  • See organisation-wide totals, never other people's details

Your data

People see what their role allows. Nothing more.

Certificate records and proof files are personal, so access is enforced where the data lives, not just in the screens.

Access
Role-based and enforced in the database. Members see only their own records; managers see only the teams they manage.
Proof files
Private storage. Files open only through short-lived links, after a permission check.
Accounts
No public sign-up. An admin creates every account.
Totals
Organisation-wide numbers are shown without names, and small groups are hidden from members.
