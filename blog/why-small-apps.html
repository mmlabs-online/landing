Most work software tries to do everything. It starts as a calendar, then adds chat, then tasks, then documents, then a dashboard about all of the above. Somewhere along the way, the part you actually opened it for gets harder to find.

We took the opposite route. Every mmlabs app is built around one everyday job, and we try to keep it that way.

If we can't describe an app in one sentence, it is doing too much.

One sentence per app

That is the test we hold ourselves to. Here is how the current family reads:

  • CaleChip helps you manage your calendar.
  • Ricotdin records and transcribes, so what was said is easy to find later.
  • SeatMGT keeps track of locations and devices.
  • CertTracker keeps your certificates and skills in one place.

What small gets you

You learn it in minutes. An app with one job has fewer screens, fewer settings and fewer places to get lost. You can open it for the first time and get something done.

It can change quickly. When an app does one thing, improving that thing does not risk breaking ten others. Feedback turns into updates faster.

You only take what you need. Need a calendar but not a transcription tool? Use one and ignore the other. Nothing is bundled in that you have to work around.

What it costs, honestly

Small apps come with trade-offs, and it would be unfair to pretend otherwise.

The first is switching. More apps can mean more tabs and more sign-ins. That is exactly why we are building one mmlabs account that works across all of them, so moving between apps feels like moving between rooms in the same house.

The second is that your information lives in more than one place. We think that is often a good thing, since each app only holds what it needs. It also means we have to think carefully about how the apps work together, and we are.

💡

Our rule of thumb: keep each app focused, and make the space between apps as small as possible.

What should we build next?
